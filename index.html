How the model works
  • Car following. Every car follows the one ahead with the Intelligent Driver Model, a standard traffic model. It sets how hard a driver brakes or accelerates from the gap, their own speed and how fast they are closing in.
  • Reaction time. Drivers act on what the gap and the car ahead looked like a moment ago, not what they look like now. Late reactions mean harder braking and more stopping.
  • Start-up lag. A stopped driver waits after the car ahead starts moving. Each car in the queue adds its own wait, which is why the queue drains slowly.
  • Pause at the front. The braking car stops, holds for the pause you chose, then pulls away like any other car.
  • Random slowdowns. Brief lifts off the gas at random. With enough cars, these alone can start a jam.
  • Stopped means under 5 mph. Longest wait is the longest unbroken stop a car has had since the last tap. Drivers differ by about 20% in reaction time and lag.
